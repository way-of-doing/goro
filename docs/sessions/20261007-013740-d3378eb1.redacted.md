**What actually happens with `(ANY(s)) AS NUMBER != 1`:**

1. **Parse.** The tree is `Comparison(Left: As(Paren(Modifier(ANY, s)), NUMBER), !=, Right: 1)`. `AS` binds more tightly than `!=`, so the left operand of `!=` is the whole conversion, and `ANY` sits inside it.
2. **Binding the `!=` operand.** `Binder.Comparison` (`Binder.cs:348`) calls `Operand(comparison.Left)` (`Binder.cs:67`). That method peels parentheses and modifiers off the outside of an operand, but the outermost node here is the `AsSyntax`, so there's nothing to peel. The quantifier stays null, and the core is the whole `AS` expression.
3. **Binding the `AS`.** Inside `Operand`, `Value(core)` reaches `As` (`Binder.cs:242`). 4. **Building the operand.** Back in `Operand`, the result is `SemanticOperand(core: the AS expression, expression: the conversion, quantifier: null)` (`Binder.cs:103`). That is the only `SemanticOperand` involved, and it stands for the whole `(ANY(s)) AS NUMBER`.
5. **The cardinality analysis**, a separate pass after binding, makes the second report. `NotEqual` (`Cardinality.cs:95`) asks `NeedsQuantifier` (`Cardinality.cs:113`) about that operand. - The quantifier isn't attached to a `SemanticOperand` and then reported. It's reported and discarded at the syntax level inside `As`, before any semantic node exists for it.

**The root cause** is that the binder's usual way of avoiding repeat errors doesn't work here. Elsewhere it gives a broken node the error type, which makes every later rule skip it.
